Commissioned by the Taiwan Earthquake Science Center Newsletter, this article explains the
findings of the 2006 Pingtung offshore earthquake doublet study to a non-specialist audience.

Earthquakes are usually thought to happen only in the cold, brittle crust, yet these two
nucleated in the mantle of the continental lithosphere. The piece starts from why rocks break
at all, then describes how earthquake source analysis and numerical modelling together imply
that thinned continental mantle remains strong enough to host earthquakes — and what that means
for hazard assessment in Taiwan.
