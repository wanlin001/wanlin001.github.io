I work at the interface between **observational seismology, structural geology and geodynamic
modelling**. The common thread is lithospheric rheology: how strong the crust and mantle are,
where that strength varies, and how those variations control where earthquakes nucleate and how
far they run.

Slab–slab interaction and mantle flow beneath Taiwan
======

Taiwan sits where the Manila and Ryukyu subduction systems meet with opposite polarity. I build
3-D geodynamic models of this double-subduction geometry, convert the modelled mantle flow into
crystallographic fabric and then into predicted seismic anisotropy, and compare that prediction
with observed SKS splitting. Because the fabric integrates the flow history, matching the
observations constrains the tectonic evolution rather than just the present-day state.

*Tools: ASPECT, D-Rex, GMT/ParaView. Manuscript in preparation; presented at EGU 2026.*

Strength of the continental lithospheric mantle
======

The 2006 Pingtung offshore earthquake doublet ruptured normal and strike-slip faults within
thinned continental lithosphere. By combining finite-fault source analysis with 800-km-scale
thermal-mechanical subduction models using nonlinear elasto-plastic-viscous rheology, I showed
that intraslab stress is strongly heterogeneous and that faulting can occur in continental
mantle — a direct constraint on lithospheric strength.

*Hu et al. (2025), Communications Earth & Environment.*

Structural and lithological control on surface deformation
======

In the mudstone-rich fold-thrust belt of southwestern Taiwan, geodetic surface deformation can
be explained either by fault geometry or by weak lithology. Thermo-mechanical finite-element
models tied to geodetic and structural observations separate the two, which matters for how
regional seismic hazard is assessed.

*Hu & Tan (2026), Tectonophysics.*

Slip-rate discrepancies and interpretation uncertainty
======

My doctoral work addressed the long-standing mismatch between geological and geodetic slip
rates in the Himalaya. A duplex-kinematics model reconciles the apparent surplus in the seismic
moment budget without invoking missing strain. A companion study quantified how much of the
scatter in geological slip rates simply comes from subjective interpretation of seismic images.

*Hu & Stevens (2022), Geology; Hu (2022), Solid Earth.*


Methods
======

* **Geodynamic / numerical modelling** — ASPECT, DynEarthSol, FLAC, MOOSE/PETSc
* **Seismology** — finite-fault waveform inversion, focal mechanism and stress inversion
* **Structural geology** — seismic interpretation, cross-section restoration, uncertainty quantification
* **Computing** — Python, MATLAB, Fortran, C++, R; Slurm/PBS, MPI/OpenMP, CUDA
* **Visualisation & GIS** — GMT, ParaView, VisIt, QGIS, ArcGIS

All model input files for published work are archived on Zenodo.
